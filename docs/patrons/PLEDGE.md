## The idea in one paragraph

Patronage pays for software that exists. A pledge pays for software that should. People promise money toward a clearly specified thing; nobody is charged unless enough is promised to build it; a builder claims the job; money is released as milestones are met; anything not delivered is refunded. It is the same commons, one step earlier.

## How it works

| Step | What happens | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Need | A stated need with a short spec: what it does, who it is for, what "done" means, the license it will ship under | Anyone; ideally starts from an existing World Issue Tracker issue or petition |
| 2. Threshold | A target amount and a deadline | The proposer, or a builder who has quoted it |
| 3. Pledge | People pledge an amount. A card is saved, not charged | Anyone |
| 4. Claim | A builder claims the job, with a plan, milestones and a public track record | A builder |
| 5. Trigger | If pledges reach the threshold by the deadline **and** a builder has claimed it, pledges are charged. Otherwise nothing is charged | Automatic |
| 6. Milestones | Money is held and released per milestone, each with a stated check | A named reviewer, not the builder |
| 7. Refund | Unmet milestones are refunded in proportion | Automatic |

### This is an assurance contract

The rule "charged only if the threshold is reached" is an assurance contract: it removes the fear of paying for something that then never gets enough support.

### The dominant-assurance variant (consider)

In a plain assurance contract, pledging when the goal fails costs nothing and gains nothing, so people wait to see who else pledges. In the dominant-assurance variant (Alex Tabarrok's proposal) the proposer adds a stake: if the threshold is **not** reached, every pledger gets their money back **plus a small bonus** from that stake. Pledging becomes the better move either way, which pulls early pledges in.

- Cost: someone must fund the failure bonus. That could be the builder (as a sign of confidence), a sponsor, or a slice of the patron pool set aside for it.
- Risk: people pledging to collect the bonus on projects they expect to fail. Limit the bonus to a small percentage, cap it per person, and pay it only to pledges made before a cut-off.
- Recommendation: launch with the plain version; trial the bonus on one or two pledges with a sponsor's money and publish what happened.

## What a good pledge needs

| Part | Rule |
|---|---|
| Spec | One page. What it does, what it will not do, what "done" means in checks someone else can run |
| License | Named up front, and open. A pledge funds a commons, not a product |
| Builder claim | A named person or team, their plan, their previous work, and what they stake |
| Milestones | Three to five. Each has a check and a share of the money |
| Reviewer | Named before the trigger. Not the builder, not paid by the builder |
| Refunds | Automatic and proportional for unmet milestones. Stated before anyone pledges |
| Record | Every pledge total, charge, release and refund appears in the same public ledger as patronage |

## Where needs come from: World Issue Tracker

People already state needs on worldissuetracker.com, including the "Missing Civilization Infrastructure" board that Humanity Infrastructure shows as "Claim a quest", and in its petitions. A pledge should not be a second place to say the same thing.

- A pledge is **attached to an issue or petition**: the issue holds the need and the discussion; the pledge holds the spec, the money and the milestones.
- An issue page shows "Pledged: $X of $Y" and a button to pledge; the pledge page links back.
- Votes and "I'd build this" signals on the issue are the early evidence that a threshold is reachable. They are shown beside the pledge, not counted as money.
- "Claim a quest" becomes the builder claim.

## How it meets the rest of the patrons site

| Existing piece | Connection |
|---|---|
| Nominate | "It does not exist yet" becomes an option on the nomination form and leads to a pledge draft |
| Have a say | Voices and thanks on a pledge are the argued case for building it; the Court map's claim is "this should exist and this plan would deliver it" |
| A project's stated need | A need that is a feature ("we need an importer") can be turned into a pledge on an existing project |
| Patron subscription | A patron may point part of their monthly amount at open pledges; it is held like any other pledge and returned to their split if the pledge fails |
| Ledger | One ledger for patronage and pledges |
| When it ships | The finished project enters the catalog and becomes eligible for patronage like any other |

## What must be true before any money moves

- A payment account and terms that allow holding and refunding money. Saving a card and charging later is a different arrangement from a subscription, and holding funds for others may be regulated. This needs proper advice, not a guess.
- A fiscal sponsor or entity that can hold funds (the same open question as patronage).
- A written refund policy and a named reviewer per pledge.
- Agreement with whoever runs World Issue Tracker on the link between issues and pledges.
