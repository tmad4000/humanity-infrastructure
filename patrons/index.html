How it works

A subscription that follows use

  1. 1

    You pay one flat amount

    One subscription covers every project in the catalog. You do not pay per app or per seat.

  2. 2

    It is shared by usage

    By default the money is divided by how much each project is actually used, from aggregate counts. Nothing is tracked per patron.

  3. 3

    You can overrule the numbers

    If one project matters more to you than its usage shows, move more of your own share to it. Your split, your call.

  4. 4

    Everything is published

    Each month we publish what came in, what the card processor took, and what went to each project.

Who can receive money Proposed rule

A project receives money when its source is public under an open license and its maintainers have agreed to take part. Today 16 of the 94 listed projects meet the license test. Most of the Ideaflow suite is free to use but not open yet, and many public repositories still need a license. The catalog shows where each one stands.

      Your own split is always yours. Voices and reasons would shape only the default, for patrons who chose nothing, alongside measured use. Counts weigh little on their own, because they are easy to give and not hard to fake. No money moves on any of this yet.

          For app makers

          If you maintain free software

          You should not have to run a fundraiser to be paid for work people use every day.

          1. 1

            Be open

            Public source and an open license. A repository without a license file does not count yet, because nobody may legally reuse it.

          2. 2

            Say yes

            Nothing is collected in your project's name without your agreement. You can decline, or leave later, and the listing is removed.

          3. 3

            Share a count, if you want

            The default split follows aggregate use. Bring a number you already publish, such as downloads or active installs. No new tracking is asked of you, and projects without a count still receive what patrons choose to give them.

          4. 4

            Get paid, in public

            Payouts appear in the monthly ledger. How money reaches you (a sponsor page you already have, or a transfer) is settled with you before the first payment.

          Where this stands today

          No money is moving yet and payout terms are not written. If you maintain something that belongs here, nominate it and leave your email; we will write to you before anything is offered in your project's name.

          The long arc

          From a few patrons to a way of funding the commons

          Open source is a feature: you can read it, change it and keep it. It deserves a funding model as simple as the software is free.

          1. Now

            Patrons

            Individuals choosing a small flat amount, starting at $1. Today you can save your place; payments open next. The goal of the first year is to show that the split is fair and the ledger is honest.

          2. Next

            Grants and matching

            Foundations, companies and public funders can add to the same pool and let patrons' choices direct it. A grant that follows thousands of small signals is hard to capture and easy to audit.

          3. Next

            Crypto communities

            Public-goods funding already has a home there: matching rounds, retroactive funding and streaming payments. We plan to accept on-chain contributions and publish the same ledger. Not live

          4. Later

            A discretionary share of public money

            Imagine a small part of your taxes being yours to direct. If the subway is failing you, you move some of that share to the community composting project that works. Versions of this exist: participatory budgeting in many cities, and Italy's rule letting taxpayers direct 0.5% of income tax to a nonprofit they choose. Software is a good place to learn how to do it well.

          Caveats

          What is true today

          Is this tax-deductible?

          No. Deductibility in the United States needs a 501(c)(3) nonprofit or a fiscal sponsor that is one. Neither is in place. Until that changes, treat a contribution as a payment, not a charitable gift.

          How is usage measured?

          This page itself carries no analytics. For the split, only from aggregate counts our sites already collect through Ideaflow's own analytics, which is anonymous, sets no cookies and respects Do Not Track. We do not add tracking for this, we do not build a usage profile per patron, and projects without counts are not guessed at. They receive money through patrons' own choices. The privacy policy for this is not final.

          Who holds the money?

          Not decided. At the start it would be received by Ideaflow, the company that maintains this site, and paid out in full after card fees. The plan is to move it to a nonprofit or fiscal sponsor.

          Have the listed projects agreed?

          Not yet. Projects made by other people are listed because they are part of this commons. Each will be asked before any money is offered in its name, and any can decline or be removed.

          What do you store when I save my place?

          Your email, the level and amount you chose, your own split if you made one, whether you asked to be told when patronage opens, the time, and the page you sent it from. A nomination stores the project, your reason and your email if you gave one. A voice stores the project, how you relate to it, your comment and name if you gave them, and a salted hash of your network so each person counts once; that hash is kept as long as the voice is. A voice has no email, so to have one removed, quote the project and your comment when you write. Lists you make and files you scan stay in your browser. It is kept in Humanity Infrastructure's own private database, hosted on Cloudflare, which also hosts this site. No separate form or marketing service is used. It is not public, not sold and not shared, and saving your place does not add you to the newsletter. To limit abuse we keep a salted hash of your network address for about a day, never the address itself. To have your entry deleted, write to jacob@ideaflow.io.

          Can I pay today?

          No. Nothing can be charged from this page yet. Leaving your email tells us which level you would choose. We do not send anything now.

          What about Patreon?

          A Patreon page is planned as a short-term mirror for people who prefer it. Long term, patronage lives here, where the split and the ledger are.
