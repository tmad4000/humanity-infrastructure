Imagine · LocalVocal, free, funded by patrons

Let local models and agents speak and hear. Free for everyone who downloads it.

LocalVocal is a Mac app that runs entirely on your machine, so a voice conversation with a local model or a coding agent can stay confidential and can work offline. In this picture nobody buys a licence. People who rely on it are patrons of the commons, and part of their subscription reaches LocalVocal every month.

What changes for the maker

Income arrives monthly and follows use, so it grows with people who keep using the app, not with one-time sales. There is no licence server, no activation limit and no checkout to run. A user who would never have paid $9.99 still counts, if they are a patron.

What it depends on

Enough patrons existing at all. Today there are none: the patron page takes names, not payments. A free LocalVocal would be an early reason for people to become patrons, and would be exposed to that risk.

Plan B · hybrid

If fully free is too big a first step

A price can stay on part of the app while patronage flows on use. The rule that makes it fair: nobody pays twice for the same thing.

Three hybrid arrangements
ArrangementWhat stays pricedWhat patronage doesHow double-paying is avoided
Free core, priced extrasA small one-time price on some editions or features. Which ones is the makers' choice.Flows on all use of the app, free or paid.What a patron's subscription has sent to LocalVocal counts toward the price. Once it adds up to the price, the extras unlock and stay unlocked.
Pay what you wantEverything is downloadable; the checkout suggests an amount and accepts zero.Flows on use, as in the free picture.A patron sees "you already support this through your subscription" at checkout, with the amount sent so far, and zero is the default.
Price now, patronage laterToday's prices stay until patron income passes a level the makers set.Starts flowing at once and is shown publicly against that level.Past buyers keep what they bought. When the level is reached the price drops for everyone, and buyers who are patrons can mark "already paid" so their share goes to their other projects for a year.

The two rules underneath

Patronage counts as payment. Money a patron's subscription has sent to a project is credited against any price that project charges them.

A purchase counts as patronage. Someone who bought can say so, and their subscription leans away from the project for a while, toward the rest of what they use.

What is not settled

Whether a priced, closed app may receive patron money at all is an open decision on the patron page, which today proposes open-licensed projects only. Crediting patronage against a price needs the app and the patron account to recognise each other, which does not exist yet.

A picture to talk over, not a proposal to sign.
